# Exercises — Observability Engineering Made Easy (Learner Edition)

Prereq: `docker compose up -d` in `master-observability`, then open
`http://localhost:8083`. Everything you need is reachable from the home card
grid. The evidence you need to answer every checkpoint is *real* — generated by
your clicks, then read back from Jaeger / Prometheus / Loki / PostgreSQL.

Watch the playlist episode names below — each exercise is the hands-on half of
one episode.

---

## Lesson 1 — The Keystone: three pillars, one click

**Beat:** an observability demo is worthless if the three pillars live on
different planets. `/keystone` runs one checkout and shows you the log line, the
counter, and the trace of that single action.

1. Open `/keystone/run` (it auto-fires one checkout on load — mode `success`).
2. Look at the three panes:
   - **Log** — one structured line: it should contain `event=checkout.completed`,
     `flow=keystone`, `userId`, `orderId`, and a `trace_id`.
   - **Metric** — `checkout_total{outcome="success"}` and a `checkout_duration`
     bucket entry.
   - **Trace** — spans: `POST /keystone/run` (root) with children
     `payment.gateway.charge`, `db.order.insert`, `db.stock.update`.
3. Re-run with **fail** and **slow** (the small select). Watch outcome labels and
   the trace error status change.

**Checkpoint:** list the five log fields + the three child span names you saw.
Answer: which pillar showed you instant structure, and which needed a
`decision_wait` (~10 s, up to a bit over half a minute end-to-end) to
appear? Why?

## Lesson 2 — Logs: structured, labeled, queryable

**Beat:** logs are *event streams with metadata*, not free text. Loki makes a log
query a `WHERE` clause.

1. Open `/logs`. Set the slider to mixed levels and generate. The table columns
   are the log's **labels** — every row also carries `trace_id`, `span_id`,
   `userId`, `flow` (MDC fields) as JSON.
2. Confirm those same fields are on the wire: open Grafana `:3000` (admin/admin)
   → Explore → Loki (uid `loki`), query `{service_name="app"}`.
3. Now filter: `{service_name="app"} |= "level=WARN"` (or `level=ERROR`).
4. Run one `POST /keystone/run`, then in Explore filter the last line to its
   `trace_id` and confirm the `userId` matches the one you saw in the app.

**Checkpoint:** what are the *labels* vs the *fields* of a log line? Write a Loki
`LogQL` query that returns only the ERROR logs containing `flow=metrics`.

## Lesson 3 — Metrics: counters, histograms, and the RED method

**Beat:** the four questions your SLO cares about are Rate, Errors, Duration
(R.E.D.), plus stock/state. Every number below comes straight from Prometheus
(`:9090` → Graph).

1. Open `/metrics`. Generate mode `success, n=10`, then `fail, n=10`, then a
   **slow** batch (capped at 15 requests — read why on the page).
2. Read `/metrics/stats` immediately, then again ~30 s later. The rows are live
   `rate(checkout_total[1m])`-style queries; for `slow` you should see the
   duration buckets fill the p95/p50 rows (a few `ms`).
3. In Prometheus, run the same expressions yourself:
   - `rate(checkout_total[1m])`
   - `sum(rate(checkout_failure_total[5m]))`
   - `histogram_quantile(0.95, sum(rate(checkout_duration_seconds_bucket[5m])) by (le))`
4. Open Grafana → "Observability Shop — Overview". Reconcile one panel with one
   hand-typed expression.

**Checkpoint:** write the expression that answers "how many checkouts per second
failed with `card_declined` in the last 5 minutes"? Which of the three RED
letters does it answer?

## Lesson 4 — Traces: one request, many services

**Beat:** a transaction hops processes; the trace keeps the thread.

1. Open `/traces`. The list is the last kept traces (root op, services, duration,
   error, trace id). Click into one **with errors**.
2. On the detail page (`/traces/{id}`), read the timeline: root span, then the
   latency waterfall with bars; each row opens **span events/logs**.
3. Switch to Jaeger `:16686` → Search → service `observability-shop`, lookback
   1 h. Filter for an error trace, expand `db.order.insert`, view its logs, and
   note the database operation spans (`SELECT postgres.customers`,
   `SELECT postgres.products`).
4. Return to the `POST` root span and read its start time — that is the one
   "wall clock" anchor for the whole waterfall.

**Checkpoint:** name the services in a checkout trace and the span whose logs
carried the exceptionMessage. What does `trace_id` do that `order_id` cannot?

## Lesson 5 — Correlation: from a failed order to root cause

**Beat:** numbers say "it failed"; only logs+trace say *why*. Correlation is the
product of the two previous lessons.

1. Open `/correlate`. Use mode `fail` or `mixed` to generate. The table lists
   **real failed orders** from the `orders` table (their error codes) — status
   says `failed`, plus `error_code`.
2. Click "trace & logs". The Evidence pane assembles, for that order:
   - the **trace** (fetch by `trace_id` from Jaeger), navigable in Jaeger;
   - the **logs** (Loki, filtered by the same `trace_id`);
   - the **failure rate** (Prometheus, last few minutes);
   - a **root-cause** hint read from the span's `exceptionMessage`.
3. Repeat with orders whose code is `card_declined`, `timeout`, `fraud_check`.
   Notice how the root-cause hint and the child span you land on differ.

**Checkpoint:** for a `card_declined` order, which span logs carry the exception
and what piece of evidence is *only* available in the trace? Which is *only* in
the logs?

## Lesson 6 — Sampling: the economics of "everything"

**Beat:** you cannot afford 100% of traces in prod. Sampling is a *decision*.

1. Open `/sampling` and run a `mix` of 20 requests. The page explains the head
   vs tail split in plain words.
2. Open the collector config
   (`docker/otel-collector/otel-collector-config.yml`) and find the `tail_sampling`
   section: `always_keep` on error, `latency` keep for slow, `probabilistic`
   remainder, everything else dropped. This mirrors `TraceDecider` in the app.
3. Read `/sampling/stats` — the last-15-minutes table from `trace_decisions`.
   Every row is a real decision: reason `error`→kept, `slow`→kept, `sampled`→kept,
   `dropped`→not exported.
4. Check the math yourself: `docker exec obs-db psql -U postgres -d postgres -c
   "SELECT reason, kept, count(*) FROM trace_decisions GROUP BY 1,2;"` — the
   keep/drop split should match the traffic you generated.

**Checkpoint:** you run 1000 req/s, keep errors + slow, drop 80% of the rest.
Roughly how many traces per second reach the backend — and what tradeoff did you
just make?

## Lesson 7 — Alerts: from threshold to on-call record

**Beat:** dashboards are for the awake-hours report; alerts are for the 3 a.m.
loop — rule → Alertmanager → webhook → persistent record.

1. Read the rule file first: `docker/prometheus/alert-rules.yml`. Note
   `FailedCheckoutRateHigh` = failure ratio > 10%, `for: 30s`;
   `ShopInstanceDown` = `up == 0`, `for: 2m`.
2. Open `/alerts`. Run **stress (fail hard)** and watch the pane: it refreshes
   through Alertmanager/Prometheus states, then shows firing alert(s).
3. Verify in Prometheus `:9090` → Alerts: confirm `FailedCheckoutRateHigh` is
   green/firing and note how long after your click it started.
4. Follow the last hop: `docker exec obs-db psql -U postgres -d postgres -c
   "SELECT alert_name, status, labels FROM alert_events ORDER BY id DESC LIMIT 5;"`
   — a row exists because Alertmanager POSTed to `app:8080/internal/alert`
   (see `docker/alertmanager/alertmanager.yml`).
5. Grafana → "SLO, Alerts & Sampling" shows the same alert with its own panel.

**Checkpoint:** with the failure ratio falling to zero, how many seconds until
`FailedCheckoutRateHigh` goes back to *inactive*? Name one thing `for:` protects
you from.

## Lesson 8 — Hygiene: redact before you ship

**Beat:** logs are shipped whole, forever. Secrets must be redacted at the *call
site*, not fixed later in the UI.

1. Open `/hygiene`. Run the **safe** mode (email + password included on the
   page). Now run **leak**. In both cases the demo writes a "customer signup"
   log line into Loki.
2. Evidence pane: fetch both. The safe line reads `email=[email]`
   `password=[redacted]`; the leak line carries the real
   `email=alice@example.com password=supersecret-123` (plus the `flow=keystone`
   and `trace_id`).
3. Find the mechanism: `obs/SafeLog.java` — redaction regexes applied where the
   log is written (there is deliberately **no** logback masking provider;
   read why in the class Javadoc).

**Checkpoint:** an attacker who can run LogQL on your Loki can read the **leak**
line's password but not the **safe** line's. Which of the two modes would you
merge to main, and what would you *not* do (i.e. why not fix it in Grafana)?

## Lesson 9 — Incident drill: read, hypothesize, solve

**Beat:** on-call is not guessing — it is reading the error code, forming a
hypothesis, and *proving* it before acting.

1. Open `/incident`, generate. 20 checkouts fire (mix of failures), a solve-work
   table appears with **real failed orders**.
2. Click an order's **solve**. The page shows:
   - the order (id, product, customer, error_code, trace_id);
   - its **trace** from the failing moment;
   - the hypothesis picker — three choices: *gateway*, *timeout*, *fraud* (+
     "something else").
3. Answer according to the mapping the drill expects:
   - `card_declined` / `insufficient_funds` → **gateway**
   - `timeout` → **timeout**
   - `fraud_check` → **fraud**
   - anything else → wrong hypothesis (the drill explains *why*).
4. On a correct answer the order is flipped to `solved` in the DB and the
   reasoning is shown. On a wrong one you are told to look again at the span
   logs — you may re-run generate for more evidence, but not restart-and-pray.

**Checkpoint:** time your full incident: from reading the error_code to the
solved row. Repeat with an `insufficient_funds` order — same hypothesis as
`card_declined`? Why do they share a verdict?

## Lesson 10 — Scale: what this stack becomes

**Beat:** the demo runs 5 containers; prod runs the same shapes autoscaled.

1. Open `/scale`. It reads live from Prometheus: how many scrape targets are up,
   who they belong to, and which would need a second replica at load.
2. Grafana → "Observability Shop — Overview": count the data sources involved
   (Prometheus metrics, Jaeger traces via the Jaeger datasource, Loki logs via
   Explore) and which panel depends on each.
3. Read `docker-compose.yml`: find the `otel-collector`, the agent's
   `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` (`:4318`, HTTP) and note the *one* config
   decision (baked-in config files vs bind mounts) and why (macOS TCC).

**Checkpoint:** a platform team adopts your `quiz` of three decisions —
OTLP over 4318, tail sampling, alert→webhook. Write one sentence explaining the
upside of each.
