# how-it-works.md — the 9 inputs → one lesson system

Lesson plans fail when they're treated as a prompt: "make me a worksheet."
The fix is to treat them as a **system**: nine inputs in, one matched lesson out.

## Inputs → output (the flow)

| # | Input            | What it tells the system            | The lesson gets…              |
|---|------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Learning goal     | what success looks like             | everything aligned to that goal|
| 2 | Prior knowledge   | what learners already know          | starts where they are         |
| 3 | Learner profile   | who the learners are                | the right tone and examples   |
| 4 | Lesson sequence   | the flow and pacing                 | logical steps and timing      |
| 5 | Learning activities| the tasks learners will do          | the right activities          |
| 6 | Output requirements| formats and constraints             | the exact deliverable         |
| 7 | Assessment evidence| how learning will be shown          | matching checks for understanding |
| 8 | Accessibility     | needs and supports                  | inclusive options for every learner |
| 9 | Teacher decisions | your style, priorities, EES         | a plan that fits your class   |

## The one rule
**Nine inputs, one system.** Leave one empty and the system has to guess — and
a plan built on a guess teaches a lesson you didn't design.

## The education system (from the source picture)
The chart in the original video path shows the arc: OLD "make me a worksheet"
→ NEW "build a custom system for my class." The brackets on the right (EES,
Teacher decisions) are the two inputs that keep the system *yours*: EES is
your class's definition of success, and Teacher decisions is the context only
you can supply. Everything else the machine can draft — those two it can't.
