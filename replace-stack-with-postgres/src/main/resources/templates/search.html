Full-text search with tsvector + pg_trigram replaces Elasticsearch / Algolia

When you insert text into a tsvector, Postgres parses it, drops stop words and applies linguistic stemming — "running" becomes "run". You query it with tsquery, rank hits with ts_rank and highlight them with ts_headline. Turn on fuzzy matching and the pg_trigram extension breaks words into three-letter chunks, so even a typo like "Postgres" (two s's) still finds "PostgreSQL". No second search cluster needed.

The engine behind this page

-- ranking + highlighting, powered by a GIN index:
SELECT title, ts_rank(search_vector, websearch_to_tsquery('english', :q)) AS rank,
       ts_headline('english', body, websearch_to_tsquery('english', :q),
                   'StartSel=<mark>, StopSel=</mark>') AS snippet
FROM articles
WHERE search_vector @@ websearch_to_tsquery('english', :q)
ORDER BY rank DESC;

-- typo-tolerant fuzzy fallback (pg_trigram, trigram word matching):
SELECT title, word_similarity(:q, title) AS sim
FROM articles
WHERE word_similarity(:q, title) > 0.4
   OR word_similarity(:q, body) > 0.4
ORDER BY sim DESC;
