## Lesson 2 — Economy & speed

**2.1**

| Proposal | Friction removed |
|---|---|
| Faster permitting & authorisation | Planning delays blocking businesses and grid/infrastructure projects |
| Banking Package / simpler fragmentation | Capital that cannot flow across borders (only ~16% of corporate lending is cross-border) |

| "Pact against gold-plating" | National governments adding extra domestic requirements on top of EU law |

**2.2** Suggested answer: "A Portuguese company today often cannot borrow from
a German bank cheaply because the European banking market is fragmented — only
about 16% of corporate lending crosses a border. That means savings made in one
member state are not financing growth in another. The Commission calls this the
crawling single market: the legal framework exists but capital, like transport
and energy, still stalls at national borders."

## Lesson 3 — Critical raw materials

**3.1** ~90% of the EU's critical raw-material imports come from China; rising
to **90%** for some rare earths. (Note: the video gives 90% for "imports of
critical minerals" and the coverage says "more than 80% dependent ... rising to
90% for some rare earths". Use 90% per the video.)

**3.2** Example chain for rare earths:
1. Depend on: wind-turbine magnets, EV motors, smartphones, precision aerospace.
2. Shock: a Chinese export ban would stall EV and defence supply lines within
   weeks — no alternative supplier at scale exists today.
3. Response: joint procurement + strategic stockpiles coordinated by a European
   body (the European Corporation on Critical Raw Materials), plus trade
   diversification and substitute development.

**3.3** In the app: lowering the slider to 70% reduces the exposure grade
(e.g. SEVERE → ELEVATED) and lengthens the stockpile "days to disruption";
raising to 95% flips the grade to CRITICAL and shrinks the stockpile clock.
Score changes because the grade is driven by single-supplier concentration and
the window to absorb a disruption.

## Lesson 4 — Defence & security

**4.1**

| Instrument | Threat | EU / NATO / both |
|---|---|---|
| €1.5bn Defence Industry Programme | Fragmented, under-scaled defence industry | EU |
| €90bn joint debt | Financing the Ukraine-support gap after frozen-Russia-assets deal stalled | EU |
| Emergency Security Protocol (Article 4 equivalent) | Hybrid threats below the threshold of armed attack | EU (flanking NATO's Article 4) |
| Counter-hybrid playbook | Sabotage, arson, drone incursions, disinformation | EU |
| European Security Council | Strategic coordination without the US | EU + non-EU partners (UK, Norway, Canada, Ukraine) |
| Instrument for Strategic Enablers | Capability gaps: missile defence, airlift, refuelling, space, cyber | EU aligned with NATO |

**4.2** Example: permanent FR, DE, IT, ES, PL + rotating NL, PT, IE → a score
weighted by population coverage and bloc balance. Swapping NL/PT for CY, MT
changes the "coverage" and "small-state representation" factors — the score
responds because legitimacy is modelled as population coverage × east-west
balance × small-state inclusion.

**4.3** The narrator notes critics say the Article 4-style mechanism is "not
nearly enough"; the sources add that it could overlap and confuse NATO
structures, and that the Security Council has no defined powers or financing.

## Lesson 5 — Energy

**5.1** Electricity's share of energy use doubles by **2040**; import bill cut
by **€260 billion/year**; **80 GW** of renewables added last year; about
**6×** that (six times) is waiting for a grid connection.

**5.2** At the 2040 target (~45–50% electricity share) the saving lands near the
video's €260bn/yr; at a ~25% target the saving is roughly half. The app's model
scales linearly, mirroring the video's framing (more electrification →
fewer fossil imports). Caveat worth naming: real-world savings also depend on
the price of gas/oil and grid build-out speed.

**5.3** Suggested answer: a connected grid lets one member state's solar power
cover another's evening peak, so the EU imports less energy and is less exposed
to price shocks and weaponised supply — that is energy security, not just
carbon reduction.

## Lesson 9 — Media literacy

**9.1** Shared facts: "the Commission invited Canada to explore associate
membership; the status does not yet exist." Frames: left-leaning sources
emphasise a warm EU–Canada partnership; right-leaning sources stress the
uncertainty of an undefined category (per the video's own Ground News debrief).

**9.2** Example positive frame: "In a historic first, the EU has opened the
door for Canada to stand shoulder-to-shoulder on defence, energy and tech."
Example sceptical frame: "The Commission announced a brand-new status for Canada
that does not exist in EU law — a promise with no legal clothes on."
(Both keep the two facts unchanged.)

**9.3** Example checklist: verify the author/date, check the primary text
(speech transcript), compare at least one left and one right source, check
who owns the outlet, and look for the original figure being cited.
